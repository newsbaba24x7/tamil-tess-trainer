Every test of tam_v3, the open Tesseract model for printed Tamil, against Tesseract's stock Tamil model, the previous open model tam_v2, the two research models trained before them, and a third-party Tamil model from Project Anuvaad. The handwriting, camera-photo tests compare tam_v3 with the stock models and the Anuvaad model. None of our models was trained on any of these test sets; for the Anuvaad model this cannot be checked. Summary

  • On real scanned books tam_v3 is the most accurate model tested. On 197 real pages it makes 46% fewer character errors and 39% fewer word errors than the stock model (16% and 8% fewer than tam_v2), and is better than stock on 179 pages and worse on none.
  • At normal scanning resolution the gain holds on every kind of page: easy, medium and hard pages, old print with pre-1978 letter shapes, and real lines read one at a time (CER 1.13%, tam_v2 1.49%).
  • RANLP 2025: tam_v3 beats Google Document AI on BLEU (0.5825 against 0.5803), ties it on ANLS (0.9923) and comes close on CER (0.80% against 0.78%); Document AI still leads on WER and METEOR, and V2-research has the lowest WER. The set is mostly Tamil with its vowel signs stripped; on its natural-Tamil lines tam_v3's CER is 0.02%.
  • English words inside Tamil: on the Tamil OCR Benchmark tam_v3 halves tam_v2's character errors (1.54% against 3.25%); nearly all of its remaining errors are in the English words.
  • Our scoring reproduces the paper's: the stock Ubuntu model scores within 0.1 points of the paper's Tesseract CER and within 0.003 of its BLEU, ANLS and METEOR.
  • Scanning resolution: tam_v3 keeps its accuracy down to 200 DPI (for 12-pt text); below that every model's errors rise steeply: at 72 DPI tam_v3's CER is 22.64% on the real pages and 43.20% on old books (stock: 15.01% and 36.22%), so at that resolution the stock model is the better choice. tam_v3 was trained on scans at normal resolution; very small text is its weak spot.
  • Handwriting: no model here was trained on it, and all read it poorly, but tam_v3 makes the fewest character errors on both handwriting tests: CER 53.45% on handwritten words (stock 59.94%, Anuvaad 75.45%) and 44.82% on handwritten lines (stock 52.75%, Anuvaad 68.28%).
  • Camera photos of documents: on 1,255 lines from 55 phone photos tam_v3's CER is 9.17%, against 11.83% for the stock model and 13.20% for Anuvaad. Printed documents read well (trade documents, book pages and papers under 3%); handwritten notes and whiteboards do not.
  • tam_v3_fast scores within about 0.4 points of tam_v3 on every test set and is 28% faster.
  • Anuvaad tam, a third-party model, is weaker than the stock model on real book pages and lines but better on RANLP 2025. It leads the Tamil OCR Benchmark because it reads English words far better than any other model here; on that benchmark's Tamil letters it and tam_v3 are almost equal (see that section). It is also the slowest model here, at about 3.0 seconds per page.

Measures

CER
Character error rate: insertions, deletions and substitutions needed to turn the output into the true text, divided by its length. Lower is better.
WER
Word error rate: the same, counted in words. Tamil words average about 8 characters, so one wrong letter or space makes the whole word wrong. Lower is better.
Tamil letters only
CER on Tamil characters alone, ignoring spaces, punctuation and digits, which proofreaders and printers handle differently.
BLEU
Share of matching word sequences of 1 to 4 words, averaged over lines (Papineni et al. 2002, as NLTK computes it). Higher is better, 0 to 1.
ANLS
Average normalised Levenshtein similarity: per line 1 − normalised edit distance, or 0 when the distance is 0.5 or more (Biten et al. 2019). Higher is better.
METEOR
Word matches weighted towards recall, with a penalty for words out of order (Banerjee & Lavie 2005, as NLTK computes it). Higher is better.

CER and WER are the main measures on this page. BLEU and METEOR were made for machine translation and count whole words, so they miss the letter-level errors that Tamil OCR makes; ANLS comes from document question answering and scores any text with half or more of it wrong as 0. They are given mainly to compare with the RANLP 2025 paper, which reports them.

Unless a section says otherwise, text is compared after Unicode NFC normalisation with invisible zero-width characters removed. RANLP 2025 is compared as is, as in its paper.

Page segmentation modes (PSM)

Before Tesseract reads any letters, it splits the image into blocks, lines and words. The page segmentation mode (--psm) tells it what kind of image to expect; the model then reads only the pieces this layout step hands it. So the mode decides what gets read at all, and the model decides how well. The modes on this page:

--psm 3
Fully automatic page layout, Tesseract's default. It looks for columns, paragraphs and lines and reads them in order, which suits book pages. Used for the page tests. On a cover or poster it often takes a large or decorated title for a picture and skips it.
--psm 11
Sparse text: find as much text as possible, anywhere, in no particular order. It suits covers, posters and signs, but also reads parts of pictures as letters.
--psm 7
The whole image is one line of text. Tesseract still checks that it can find a text line; on very faint or damaged lines that check can fail, and the output is empty.
--psm 13
A raw line: the whole image is one line, without that check. Used for the real-line, handwriting and camera-photo tests.

Other modes include 6 (one uniform block of text) and 12 (sparse text with orientation detection); tesseract --help-psm lists them all.

Anuvaad tam leads here because it reads the English words: on the 686 English letters (in 52 of the 200 lines) it gets 1.31% wrong, against 26.68% for tam_v3. The stock model cannot write English at all: its character set has no Latin letters, so it turns English words into Tamil letters and digits, and so do V1 and V2-research, which kept that character set; tam_v2 and tam_v3 added Latin letters. On the Tamil letters alone, Anuvaad and tam_v3 are almost equal (0.06% and 0.10%). The lines are clean renders in a single font, Noto Sans Tamil, which is also among the training fonts of our models, so the test says little about real scans, where Anuvaad is weaker than the stock model.

tam_v3 can still improve a lot here, and part of the gain needs no new model. With --psm 7, 50% of its errors come from 23 lines it returns empty, as every model does on nearly the same lines: very thin letters or heavy damage, where Tesseract's own line check finds no text line. --psm 13 (raw line) skips that check and reads them, which brings tam_v3 to 1.52%; the real-line tests on this page all use --psm 13. The rest is the model's: on the lines it does read with --psm 7 its CER is 1.13%, and in the three font families no model was trained on (63 lines) it is 3.71% with --psm 13. Rendered lines are easier than real scans, and a model can improve here without improving on real pages, as V1 did. tam_v3's rendering text includes the books this set's text was drawn from, so for tam_v3 only the three held-out font families are fully unseen.

Scanning resolution

How small can the text be? Each page is read as scanned, and again after being shrunk to what a scanner set to 300, 200, 150, 100 or 72 DPI would capture of 12-pt print. Scans rarely record their true resolution, so each page's body-text line height is measured first (tools/make_dpi_set.py) and pages printed in larger type are shrunk further: what a model sees is the size of the letters in pixels. At 12 pt a text line is about 57 pixels tall at 300 DPI, 38 at 200, 28 at 150, 19 at 100 and 14 at 72.

Pages are only ever made smaller, never enlarged: each new pixel is the average of the source pixels it covers, as in a scanner's sensor. Every column has the same pages, so the columns can be compared; a page whose source text is too small for 300 DPI is left out of all of them. Pages whose text belongs to another page are found as scanned and left out; every other page counts, however badly it reads.

CER and WER are given at every resolution. BLEU, ANLS and METEOR follow the same pattern and are given as scanned and at 72 DPI only; each page is scored as one text and the scores are averaged over the pages.

Handwriting

None of the models under this section should be used to read photographs of handwritten text (notes, letters, forms filled in by hand, whiteboards). The reasons:

  • Most letters come out wrong. In the camera-photo test below, on phone photos of handwritten notes tam_v3 gets 37.88% of the letters wrong (stock 46.35%, Anuvaad 55.79%), and on whiteboards and blackboards 43.74% (stock 57.65%, Anuvaad 57.22%): more than one letter in three, whichever model reads them. Text like that takes longer to correct than to type again.
  • They learnt printed letters only. Every model here was trained on printed fonts, and tam_v3 also on scanned printed books; the Anuvaad model's training data is not published. Handwritten letters change from writer to writer (slant, joined or broken strokes, uneven sizes, loops), and the models never saw those shapes. Even on clean, cut-out handwritten words and lines (the tests below) the character error rate is 45–75%, and the best model reads only 4.3% of words exactly.
  • A photo adds what the models were not trained for. They learnt from flat, evenly lit scans. A phone photo adds blur, shadows, uneven light, an angled view, paper texture and ruled lines, which make the thin, uneven strokes of handwriting harder still to separate from the background.
  • Lines are hard to find. On a whole photo Tesseract must first find the lines of text, and handwritten lines slope, touch and run into each other. The tests on this page cut the lines out first, so a whole photo reads worse.
  • Words get split. The gaps in handwriting are uneven, so the models put spaces inside words: on handwritten lines every model writes more words than the lines have (word error rate 143% or more).

Reading handwriting would need a Tesseract model trained on handwritten Tamil lines.

Reproduce these results

All test lists, the scoring code and the commands are in the repository; the full tables with every line set are in benchmarks/BASELINE.md.

git clone https://github.com/newsbaba24x7/tamil-tess-trainer && cd tamil-tess-trainer
R=https://github.com/newsbaba24x7/tamil-tess-trainer/releases/download/v3.0.0
mkdir -p models && curl -L -o models/tam_v3.traineddata $R/tam_v3.traineddata
C="--compare tam_v3=models/tam_v3.traineddata"

# 197 real pages (slow: Wikimedia limits image downloads)
python3 tools/fetch_wikisource.py --from-list examples/wikisource-pages.json --out scans/wikisource
python3 -m tamtrain scans scans/wikisource --name scans $C

# real lines, rebuilt from the release's line list
curl -LO $R/tam_v3-real-lines.jsonl.gz
python3 tools/crop_lines.py tam_v3-real-lines.jsonl.gz --out real-lines --split test
python3 -m tamtrain lines real-lines/test --name real-test-lines --psm 13 $C

# RANLP 2025, scored as in the paper
curl -L -o data.arrow https://huggingface.co/datasets/Nevidu/tamil_synthetic_ocr/resolve/main/data/data-00000-of-00001.arrow
python3 tools/hf_arrow_to_lines.py data.arrow --out benchmarks/ranlp2025/lines
python3 -m tamtrain lines benchmarks/ranlp2025/lines --name ranlp2025 --raw --psm 3 $C

# scanning resolution: the real pages and the old-book pages, shrunk to 300, 200, 150, 100 and 72 DPI
python3 tools/fetch_wikisource.py --from-list benchmarks/resolution/old-books-source/pages.json --out old-books
python3 -m tamtrain scans old-books --name old-books-source $C
for s in scans/wikisource:real-pages old-books:old-books; do
  python3 tools/make_dpi_set.py ${s%%:*} --out res/${s##*:} --dpi 72 100 150 200 300
done
for d in 300 200 150 100 072; do
  python3 -m tamtrain scans res/real-pages/dpi-$d --name real-pages-$d --exclude-from work/report/scans.json $C
  python3 -m tamtrain scans res/old-books/dpi-$d --name old-books-$d --exclude-from work/report/old-books-source.json $C
done

# handwriting: IIIT-INDIC-HW-WORDS, Tamil test split, then the same words joined into lines
python3 tools/iiit_hw_words.py fetch test --out downloads/iiit-hw
python3 tools/iiit_hw_words.py convert downloads/iiit-hw/test.zip --labels downloads/iiit-hw/test.txt     --vocab downloads/iiit-hw/vocab.txt --out hw-words/test
python3 -m tamtrain lines hw-words/test --name iiit-hw-test-words --psm 13 $C
python3 tools/join_hw_words.py hw-words/test --out hw-lines/test
python3 -m tamtrain lines hw-lines/test --name iiit-hw-test-lines --psm 13 $C

# camera photos: download and unpack the Appen sample from Kaggle into appen-photos/
python3 tools/labelme_lines.py appen-photos --out appen-lines
python3 -m tamtrain lines appen-lines --name appen-photo-lines --psm 13 $C

Reports go to work/report/ and always include the stock tam model. Add more --compare label=path/to/model.traineddata options for more models. Each line report also saves every line's OCR text.
