Summary

  • On real scanned books tam_v2 is the most accurate model tested. On 197 real pages it makes 36% fewer character errors and 33% fewer word errors than the stock model, and is better on 173 pages and worse on 1.
  • At normal scanning resolution the gain holds on every kind of page: easy, medium and hard pages, old print with pre-1978 letter shapes, and real lines read one at a time.
  • RANLP 2025: Google Document AI still leads on four of the five measures. V2-research has the lowest word error rate; V1 comes closest on the others. The set is mostly Tamil with its vowel signs stripped, so tam_v2's knowledge of real Tamil word boundaries costs it words here; on the natural-Tamil lines its CER is 0.07%.
  • Our scoring reproduces the paper's: the stock Ubuntu model scores within 0.1 points of the paper's Tesseract CER and within 0.003 of its BLEU, ANLS and METEOR.
  • Weak spots: very low resolution (72–96 DPI), where every model's errors roughly triple, and English words inside Tamil lines.
  • tam_v2_fast scores within about 0.4 points of tam_v2 everywhere and is 28% faster.

Measures

CER
Character error rate: insertions, deletions and substitutions needed to turn the output into the true text, divided by its length. Lower is better.
WER
Word error rate: the same, counted in words. Tamil words average about 8 characters, so one wrong letter or space makes the whole word wrong. Lower is better.
Tamil letters only
CER on Tamil characters alone, ignoring spaces, punctuation and digits, which proofreaders and printers handle differently.
BLEU
Share of matching word sequences of 1 to 4 words, averaged over lines (Papineni et al. 2002, as NLTK computes it). Higher is better, 0 to 1.
ANLS
Average normalised Levenshtein similarity: per line 1 − normalised edit distance, or 0 when the distance is 0.5 or more (Biten et al. 2019). Higher is better.
METEOR
Word matches weighted towards recall, with a penalty for words out of order (Banerjee & Lavie 2005, as NLTK computes it). Higher is better.

Unless a section says otherwise, text is compared after Unicode NFC normalisation with invisible zero-width characters removed. RANLP 2025 is compared as is, as in its paper.

Reproduce these results

All test lists, the scoring code and the commands are in the repository; the full tables with every line set are in benchmarks/BASELINE.md.

git clone https://github.com/newsbaba24x7/tamil-tess-trainer && cd tamil-tess-trainer
R=https://github.com/newsbaba24x7/tamil-tess-trainer/releases/download/v2.0.0
mkdir -p models && curl -L -o models/tam_v2.traineddata $R/tam_v2.traineddata
C="--compare tam_v2=models/tam_v2.traineddata"

# 197 real pages (slow: Wikimedia limits image downloads)
python3 tools/fetch_wikisource.py --from-list examples/wikisource-pages.json --out scans/wikisource
python3 -m tamtrain scans scans/wikisource --name scans $C

# real lines, rebuilt from the release's line list
curl -LO $R/tam_v2-real-lines.jsonl.gz
python3 tools/crop_lines.py tam_v2-real-lines.jsonl.gz --out real-lines
python3 -m tamtrain lines real-lines/test --name real-test-lines --psm 13 $C

# RANLP 2025, scored as in the paper
curl -L -o data.arrow https://huggingface.co/datasets/Nevidu/tamil_synthetic_ocr/resolve/main/data/data-00000-of-00001.arrow
python3 tools/hf_arrow_to_lines.py data.arrow --out benchmarks/ranlp2025/lines
python3 -m tamtrain lines benchmarks/ranlp2025/lines --name ranlp2025 --raw --psm 3 $C

Reports go to work/report/ and always include the stock tam model. Add more --compare label=path/to/model.traineddata options for more models. Each line report also saves every line's OCR text.
